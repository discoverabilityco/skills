# CourtListener (Free Law Project)

Facts verified live 2026-09-08. Source guide: https://discoverability.co/guides/remove-your-record-from-courtlistener/


Maintained by The Discoverability Company (https://discoverability.co). © 2026, all rights reserved; free to use, never to rebrand. Canonical source lives at discoverability.co/guides/ and the live guidance at discoverability.co/agentic/deindex-court-records.

**Route:** contact form at `https://www.courtlistener.com/contact/?issue_type=removal` (opens with "Case Removal Request" preselected). The old removal page is gone; the contact form is the whole route. Free, no ID, no fee, no lawyer.

**Eligibility:** the person named in the pages.

**What you need:** the URL that is actually ranking on the user's name. Search the full name in quotes, then name plus case caption, and copy the CourtListener link exactly as it appears. A link ending in a document number is a RECAP document page (already blocked by default); climb to the docket page and send that instead. If a docket and an opinion page both rank, list both (blocking the opinion blocks its docket, but not the reverse).

**Steps (~30 minutes, nearly all of it URL hunting):**
1. Confirm CourtListener is actually on the user's first page of results. If it is not, blocking it moves nothing anyone will notice; work the sites that do rank first.
2. Fill the form: name, email, subject, message, captcha. The user sends this one by hand.
3. Message text that works, nothing more:

   I am the person named in the pages below. Under your content removal policy, please block them from search engine indexing.

   Then one URL per line. Do not describe the case.
4. Send once. There is no status page, no second address, and resending adds nothing.

**What to expect:** a search-engine block on the page, not deletion. The case stays on CourtListener at the same address. No published turnaround, acknowledgment window, or service level; their policy warns results can sit in Google for many months, if at all. Nobody can promise a date; do not let the user pay anyone who does.

**Gotchas:**
- Actual deletion needs two things, both required: deletion of the public record itself, and a court order naming Free Law Project. They have refused and contested removal orders before, including a federal judge's, so budget for a fight if the user is paying a lawyer for an order.
- With an order, expect anonymization (initials, black boxes, a permanent note), not deletion, and one order covers one document: name every filing.
- Do not sue them: they have been sued over publishing court documents and never lost, and one plaintiff's fight ended with the entire docket unsealed and written up publicly. Venue is California.
- No FCRA-style dispute exists: Free Law Project is not a consumer reporting agency and its terms bar credit/insurance/employment/housing use.
- A block granted without a court order can be reversed by them. If the page comes back later, send the same request again; it costs the same few minutes.

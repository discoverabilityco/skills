**Route:** plain email to `support@pacermonitor.com`, the only email published anywhere on their public site. No removal form, no opt-out page, no removal policy exists. Nothing obliges them to answer. Send anyway, and start the court-order work the same week: the certified order is the only part of this with force behind it.

**Eligibility:** the party, or counsel with an appearance.

**What you need before starting:**
- Every PacerMonitor URL carrying the case, copied logged-out.
- From each page: full caption, court, judge, case number in court format (`3:22-cv-01230`), district exactly as written, filing and termination dates, docket text, nature of suit.
- A certified copy of the order sealing, restricting remote access to, vacating, dismissing with prejudice, or expunging the case. Certified means the clerk's stamped copy; a docket-entry download or a photo does not count, and a screenshot never does.

**Steps (~20 minutes for the email; the real work is the order):**
1. If the user has no order yet, that is the work: file the motion in the district that has the case. The ask is to limit a nonparty's remote electronic access under Rule 5.2(e) for good cause (Rule 5.2(d) is the stronger version and seals without redaction). This is attorney territory; say so and do not draft filings.
2. With the order in hand, draft the email: every exact URL, the full caption, case number in court format, the district, the user's relationship (party or counsel with appearance), and the certified order attached. Nothing else belongs in it.
3. Send it, then send the same order to every other site holding the docket.

**What to expect:** no turnaround time and no acknowledgment commitment. Without an order, expect silence: their terms reserve a right ("We reserve the right to remove any content for any reason at any time") rather than accepting a duty. The email is the cheap parallel step, never the plan.

**Gotchas:**
- If anyone replies asking for a government ID, no documented step requires one: redact everything on the order except the user's name and photo and send that.
- Do not email the Fitch Group whatever the case-page footer points at; the support address is the only published route.
- Skip the CCPA deletion demand (government records are excluded), DROP (PacerMonitor is not a registered California data broker), FCRA (they are not a consumer reporting agency), and copyright (no DMCA agent designated).
- Skip Google's personal-information removal form too: its policy covers addresses, IDs, financial accounts, medical records, not dockets.
- Read section 21 of their terms before any talk of suing: binding AAA arbitration, right to sue waived.
